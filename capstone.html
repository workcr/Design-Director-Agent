Capstone Project

Synthesize all modules into one comprehensive system-driven identity or editorial artifact, documented and presented at SearchSystem™-level quality.

Project Overview

The capstone project is your opportunity to demonstrate mastery across all nine modules. This is not simply another design project—it's a comprehensive demonstration of systematic thinking, typographic precision, compositional rigor, and conceptual depth. The final deliverable should be portfolio-ready work that represents the highest level of your capabilities.

Success Criteria

Your capstone must demonstrate excellence in all areas covered by the curriculum:

  • Extreme Reduction: Minimal, essential elements only—generous negative space
  • Typographic Mastery: Perfect hierarchy, kerning, and typographic refinement
  • Grid Systems: Rigorous underlying structure, systematic layout logic
  • Professional Documentation: SearchSystem™-quality photography and presentation
  • Strategic Curation: Coherent project narrative with concise, compelling description
  • Conceptual Depth: Clear idea driving formal decisions
  • International Influences: Evidence of studying and synthesizing master designers
  • Color Discipline: Restrained, purposeful color application
  • Critical Refinement: Self-critique applied, work polished to professional standard

Project Options

Choose ONE of the following capstone project types:

Option A: System-Driven Brand Identity

Design a complete visual identity system for a cultural institution, design studio, or conceptual brand.

Requirements

  • Logo/wordmark with clear systematic logic
  • Modular or flexible identity system (minimum 3 variations)
  • 6-8 brand applications: stationery, signage, publication, website mockup, packaging, or environmental graphics
  • Comprehensive brand guidelines (8-12 pages): grid system, typography, color palette, usage rules
  • Professional documentation of all artifacts
  • Project description (150-200 words) explaining concept and system logic

Timeline

4-6 weeks | 60-80 hours

Option B: Editorial Publication System

Design a complete editorial system—magazine, journal, or book series with systematic structure.

Requirements

  • Publication concept with clear editorial vision
  • Grid system and typographic framework
  • Cover design with systematic flexibility (3 variations showing system in use)
  • 24-32 interior pages demonstrating full layout system: articles, images, captions, pull quotes, diagrams
  • Consistent design language across varied content types
  • Print-ready or high-fidelity digital mockup
  • Professional documentation and project description (150-200 words)

Timeline

4-6 weeks | 60-80 hours

Option C: Experimental Design Research Project

Self-directed research exploring a design question through systematic visual investigation.

Requirements

  • Clear research question or conceptual theme
  • Written research brief (300-500 words) explaining inquiry, precedents, methodology
  • Series of 10-15 visual explorations responding to the question
  • Systematic approach—establish rules/constraints and explore variations
  • Final presentation publication or exhibition format (16-24 pages or installation documentation)
  • Process documentation showing iterations and development
  • Project summary (200-300 words) analyzing findings and outcomes

Timeline

5-7 weeks | 70-90 hours

Option D: Comprehensive Portfolio Redesign

Curate, refine, and present your complete design portfolio at SearchSystem™ quality level.

Requirements

  • Strategic curation: 8-12 best projects, each revised to professional standard
  • Re-photography of all physical artifacts with consistent lighting and style
  • Concise project descriptions (50-100 words each) following professional standards
  • Complete portfolio website OR PDF presentation (40-60 pages)
  • Consistent design system across entire portfolio: grid, typography, color, spacing
  • Professional bio and contact information
  • Portfolio must demonstrate range while maintaining cohesive visual identity

Timeline

6-8 weeks | 80-100 hours

Process & Milestones

Week 1-2: Research & Conceptualization

  • Choose project type and define scope
  • Conduct research on precedents and references
  • Develop concept and write project brief
  • Create mood boards and initial explorations
  • Define grid system, typography, and color palette

Week 3-4: Design Development

  • Design primary applications or layouts
  • Refine system logic and establish design rules
  • Create multiple iterations and variations
  • Apply self-critique using Module 09 criteria
  • Seek external feedback from peers or mentors

Week 5-6: Refinement & Documentation

  • Polish all details: kerning, spacing, alignment, color accuracy
  • Produce final artifacts (print or high-fidelity digital)
  • Photograph all work following Module 04 standards
  • Write final project description
  • Create presentation layout or portfolio pages

Final Review Checklist

Before submitting your capstone, verify:

  • ☐ All typography is manually kerned and optically correct
  • ☐ Grid system is consistent and precisely applied
  • ☐ Color palette is intentional and restrained
  • ☐ Negative space is actively designed, not leftover
  • ☐ Every element has a clear purpose
  • ☐ Photography is professional quality with consistent lighting
  • ☐ Project description is concise, clear, and typo-free
  • ☐ Work represents your absolute best capability
  • ☐ You would be proud to show this to SearchSystem™

Submission & Presentation

Deliverables

All capstone projects must include:

  1. Final Artifacts: All designed applications, publications, or systems
  2. Professional Documentation: High-quality photographs of all physical work
  3. Project Presentation: 10-20 page PDF or web page showing complete project with context
  4. Process Documentation: 4-6 pages showing research, iterations, and development
  5. Written Components: Project description, concept rationale, design system explanation
  6. Self-Critique: 1-page reflection using Module 09 assessment criteria—what worked, what could improve

Presentation Format

Your capstone should be presented as a complete case study suitable for portfolio inclusion:

  • Clear project title and one-sentence tagline
  • Hero image establishing visual tone
  • Context and description (150-200 words)
  • System documentation (grids, typography, color, rules)
  • Application images showing work in context
  • Detail shots revealing craft and refinement
  • Process overview (optional but recommended)
  • Closing image or summary

Evaluation Standards

Your capstone will be evaluated (by yourself or mentors) on these criteria:

Conceptual Strength

Is there a clear idea? Does form support concept? Is there depth beyond aesthetics?

Systematic Rigor

Is there an underlying system? Is it consistent? Does it scale and adapt logically?

Typographic Excellence

Is hierarchy clear? Are details refined? Is kerning optically correct?

Compositional Mastery

Is the grid effective? Is negative space intentional? Is balance achieved?

Craft & Refinement

Are details perfect? Is execution flawless? Does it feel professional?

Documentation Quality

Is photography professional? Are contexts shown effectively? Is presentation cohesive?

After the Capstone

Next Steps

Upon completing your capstone project:

  • Add it as the centerpiece of your portfolio
  • Share on Behance, Dribbble, or portfolio platforms
  • Present to design communities or peers for feedback
  • Consider submitting to design competitions or publications
  • Use it as a conversation starter in job interviews or client pitches
  • Reflect on your growth throughout the 12-week program

Continued Practice

Design mastery is a lifelong pursuit. Continue to:

  • Study master designers and international movements
  • Practice typographic refinement daily
  • Maintain design discipline in all projects
  • Seek critique and external perspective
  • Balance commercial work with experimental exploration
  • Revisit modules as needed to reinforce skills
