Experimental & Conceptual Thinking

Explore speculative design, system-based art, and conceptual branding. Push beyond commercial constraints into inventive territory.

Theoretical Foundation

Design as Inquiry

Experimental design asks "what if?" rather than "how to?" It uses design methods to explore ideas, question assumptions, and propose alternative futures. This work may not have immediate commercial application, but it pushes your creative boundaries and develops conceptual depth.

Conceptual vs. Commercial

Balance is essential. Commercial work solves client problems; conceptual work solves problems you identify. The strongest designers do both—rigor from commercial practice informs experimental freedom, and conceptual thinking elevates commercial solutions.

Approaches to Experimental Work

  • Speculative Design: Imagine future scenarios and design for them (critical design, design fiction)
  • System-Based Art: Create rule-based systems that generate varied outputs (generative design, parametric systems)
  • Conceptual Branding: Invent fictional brands or institutions that explore ideas rather than sell products
  • Typographic Experimentation: Push letterforms beyond legibility to explore form and meaning
  • Material Exploration: Use unconventional materials or processes to create unexpected outcomes

Precedents & Influences

  • Designers: Anthony Dunne & Fiona Raby (speculative design), James Goggin (conceptual typography), Experimental Jetset (systematic thinking)
  • Movements: Fluxus, Situationist International, Conceptual Art
  • Contemporary: Metahaven, Dexter Sinister, OK-RM, Laurenz Brunner

Maintaining Rigor in Experimental Work

Experimental doesn't mean undisciplined:

  • Set clear rules or constraints for your system
  • Document process and iterations
  • Apply the same craft standards as commercial work
  • Develop clear conceptual rationale—why this exploration?

Weekly Exercises

EXERCISE 01

Generative Type System

Objective

Create a rule-based system that generates varied typographic compositions.

Steps

  1. Define 5-7 simple rules (e.g., "all type must align to 45° angles", "use only multiples of 10pt", "letters cannot touch").
  2. Apply these rules to create 10 different compositions using the same text.
  3. Rules stay constant; outcomes vary based on interpretation and execution.
  4. Document your system rules clearly.
  5. Reflect: what variety emerges from constraint?

Deliverables

  • 10 compositions following the same rule system
  • System documentation (rules clearly stated)
Time: 8-10 hours
EXERCISE 02

Speculative Brand Identity

Objective

Design an identity for a fictional institution that explores a conceptual idea.

Steps

  1. Invent an institution 50 years in the future: "Ministry of Time Management", "Institute for Forgetting", "Bureau of Slow Technology".
  2. Develop a complete identity: logo, stationery, signage, publication.
  3. Let the concept drive formal decisions—form follows fiction.
  4. Maintain design rigor even as concept is speculative.
  5. Write a 100-word manifesto for your institution.

Deliverables

  • Identity system (4-6 applications)
  • Institutional manifesto
  • Conceptual rationale
Time: 12-14 hours
EXERCISE 03

Constraint-Based Design Challenge

Objective

Use extreme constraints to force inventive solutions.

Steps

  1. Set severe constraints: design a poster using only 3 circles, or only vertical and horizontal lines, or only one letter from the alphabet repeated.
  2. Create 5 different posters under the same constraint.
  3. Each must communicate different content or mood despite identical limitations.
  4. Explore: how much can you express with minimal means?
  5. Compare: which constraints were most generative?

Deliverables

  • 5 posters under one constraint
  • Analysis of how constraint influenced creativity
Time: 8-10 hours
EXERCISE 04

Conceptual Publication

Objective

Design a publication that is itself a conceptual statement.

Steps

  1. Choose a conceptual theme: "A Book About Nothing", "Manual for Unlearning", "Atlas of Imaginary Places".
  2. Design 12-16 pages where form and content are inseparable.
  3. The design should embody the concept, not just illustrate it.
  4. Experiment with structure: fold-outs, die-cuts, unconventional binding, transparent pages.
  5. Photograph or mockup the final publication.

Deliverables

  • 12-16 page conceptual publication (digital or physical)
  • Concept statement (100 words)
Time: 14-16 hours
EXERCISE 05

Data Visualization as Art

Objective

Transform personal data into an aesthetic system.

Steps

  1. Track personal data for one week: hours slept, steps walked, words written, coffee consumed, moods, etc.
  2. Design a visual system to represent this data—not a conventional chart, but an aesthetic interpretation.
  3. Create a poster or publication showing one week of data.
  4. Form should reflect the nature of the data—rhythm, repetition, variation.
  5. Balance readability with aesthetic expression.

Deliverables

  • Data visualization poster or publication
  • Raw data and design system explanation
Time: 10-12 hours
EXERCISE 06

Self-Initiated Research Project

Objective

Develop a complete self-directed design research project.

Steps

  1. Identify a design question or theme you want to explore (e.g., "How can typography convey time?", "What does invisible labor look like?").
  2. Conduct research: precedents, references, related work.
  3. Develop a series of 6-8 visual explorations responding to your question.
  4. Document your process and findings.
  5. Present as a cohesive project with introduction, explorations, and conclusion.

Deliverables

  • Research documentation
  • 6-8 visual explorations
  • Project summary (200-300 words)
Time: 16-20 hours
