Project Curation & Narrative

Curate a compelling portfolio. Select projects strategically, write concise captions, and structure entries to create narrative and progression.

Theoretical Foundation

Curation as Strategic Editing

Your portfolio is not an archive—it's a curated exhibition of your best thinking. Like museums select artworks to tell a story, you must choose projects that demonstrate range, depth, and a point of view. Quality always trumps quantity.

Selection Criteria

Evaluate projects against these standards:

  • Excellence: Is this work among your absolute best? Does it represent your highest skill level?
  • Conceptual Depth: Does it show thinking beyond aesthetics? Is there a strong idea?
  • Craft: Is execution flawless? Details refined?
  • Range: Does it show a different skill or approach than other portfolio pieces?
  • Relevance: Does it align with the type of work you want to do next?

The Art of the Project Description

SearchSystem™ and top studios write minimal, precise project descriptions. Follow these principles:

  • Brevity: 50-150 words maximum. Every word must earn its place.
  • Context First: Client/project type, year, scope.
  • Problem/Solution: What challenge were you addressing? What was your approach?
  • Outcome: What was created? (Not promotional language—factual description.)
  • Tone: Professional, neutral, confident. Avoid hyperbole or self-congratulation.

Portfolio Organization Methods

  • Chronological: Show progression over time (useful early in career)
  • Categorical: Group by project type (identity, editorial, digital, experimental)
  • Reverse Chronological: Best/most recent work first (common for established designers)
  • Curated Sequence: Strategic order creating rhythm and variety (most sophisticated)

Creating Narrative Arc

Your portfolio should have flow and rhythm:

  • Lead with a strong project—set the bar high
  • Vary project types and visual styles to maintain interest
  • Avoid clustering similar projects together
  • End with a memorable piece—leave lasting impression
  • Consider pacing: intense vs. quiet, complex vs. minimal

Weekly Exercises

EXERCISE 01

Portfolio Audit & Selection

Objective

Critically evaluate your work and select the strongest projects for portfolio inclusion.

Steps

  1. Gather all completed design projects from the past 1-2 years.
  2. Evaluate each against the five selection criteria (excellence, depth, craft, range, relevance).
  3. Rate each project 1-5 on each criterion. Calculate total scores.
  4. Select the top 6-10 projects based on scores and portfolio balance.
  5. Write a brief justification (2-3 sentences) for why each made the cut.

Deliverables

  • Evaluation spreadsheet with all projects rated
  • Final selection list (6-10 projects) with justifications
Time: 4-5 hours
EXERCISE 02

Writing Concise Project Descriptions

Objective

Master the art of brief, impactful project descriptions.

Steps

  1. For each selected portfolio project, write a 100-150 word description following the structure: Context → Problem → Approach → Outcome.
  2. Edit ruthlessly—remove adjectives, eliminate redundancy, favor active voice.
  3. Compare your descriptions to SearchSystem™ or other top studios—match their tone and brevity.
  4. Have someone read your descriptions and summarize them back to you—are they clear?
  5. Refine to final 50-100 word versions.

Deliverables

  • Project descriptions for 6-10 projects (50-100 words each)
  • Notes on revision process
Time: 6-8 hours
EXERCISE 03

Portfolio Sequencing Exercise

Objective

Develop strategic project sequencing that creates rhythm and progression.

Steps

  1. Print thumbnails of all selected projects (or create digital tiles).
  2. Experiment with 3 different sequences: chronological, categorical, and curated (strategic).
  3. For the curated sequence: consider visual rhythm, project variety, pacing between complex and simple.
  4. Lead with your strongest work. End with a memorable piece.
  5. Document each sequence with rationale for the ordering logic.

Deliverables

  • Three different portfolio sequences with visual diagrams
  • Written rationale for each approach
  • Final recommendation on which sequence works best
Time: 4-6 hours
EXERCISE 04

Complete Portfolio Presentation

Objective

Assemble a complete, presentation-ready portfolio.

Steps

  1. Using your final project selection, descriptions, and sequence, create a complete portfolio presentation.
  2. Choose format: PDF document (for email), web page, or physical book.
  3. Design with consistent layout system: title page, table of contents, individual project pages with hero images + descriptions.
  4. Maintain visual consistency throughout—grid, typography, spacing.
  5. Include brief bio/statement (50 words max) and contact info.

Deliverables

  • Complete portfolio in chosen format
  • 20-40 pages (depending on project count)
  • Presentation-ready for professional sharing
Time: 12-16 hours
